DEMO 1 — ORIGINAL PAGODA DETECTION (ABOUT 3 MINUTES)
--------------------------------------------------
RUN THESE THREE COMMANDS, IN ORDER:

COMMAND MEANINGS:
- Push-Location temporarily enters the pagoda-batch71 experiment directory, where Pagoda can read/write experiment artifacts, and remembers the previous directory.
- ..\..\.venv\Scripts\python.exe launches the project's virtual-environment Python.
- ..\..\pagoda\main.py is the original Pagoda program.
- ..\batch71\train supplies 71 benign training graphs.
- ..\batch5\attack supplies five original attack graphs.
- ..\batch5\evasion supplies five mimicry-evasion graphs.
- Pop-Location returns to the original repository directory.
- ".." means the parent folder; "..\.." means two folders up.

BEFORE RUNNING, SAY:
"First, we run the original Pagoda detector. Its training data consists of 71 benign provenance graphs. 
We then test five original attacks and five mimicry-evasion graphs. The detector builds or reuses its frequency-based model and thresholds, scores activity paths and graphs, and reports how many samples were flagged."

PREVIOUSLY OBSERVED OUTPUT (CHECK ACTUAL LIVE OUTPUT):
fpr: 0
tpr: 5
evasion: 0

EXPLAIN THE RESULTS:
"These labels are counts in this implementation, not percentages. 
The fpr value of zero refers to the benign training graphs evaluated by this program; it is not an independent false-positive rate. 
The tpr value of five means all five original attack graphs were detected. The evasion value of zero means none of the five evasion graphs were detected. 
Therefore, all five selected mimicry-evasion graphs escaped detection in this 71-graph configuration."

IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION:
"This is a partial reproduction on five selected attack and evasion graphs, not proof that all attacks evade Pagoda. 
The released program's graph threshold calibration also uses attack scores, so our attack detection figure is not a fully independent test."

IF ASKED ABOUT THE THRESHOLD:
"The graph threshold in our 71-graph experiment was about 0.1237. A graph-level anomaly score above that threshold is flagged at graph level. 
Pagoda also has a separate path-level detection check. 
The threshold changed from about 0.1038 in the five-training-graph experiment, but we cannot say that threshold change alone caused the different evasion result."

IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION:
"A 100 percent false-positive rate on only five held-out samples does not mean Pagoda has a 100 percent false-positive rate in general. 
Our evaluation procedure may differ from the original paper's protocol. 
This is an observed limitation of our particular setup, not a universal claim about the detector."

CLOSING (ABOUT 45–60 SECONDS)
----------------------------
SAY:
"Our reproduction found that Pagoda detected all five original attack graphs but missed all five selected mimicry-evasion graphs when using 71 benign training graphs. 
That qualitatively supports the paper's central concern: attackers may disguise malicious activity within provenance data. 
However, our additional held-out evaluation also flagged five out of five normal graphs. 
This demonstrates why we must examine both missed attacks, called false negatives, and incorrect alerts on normal activity, called false positives. 
Because we tested only a small subset and used a custom held-out evaluation, our conclusions are limited to this setup."

QUESTIONS AND SHORT ANSWERS
---------------------------
Q: Did you write Pagoda yourselves?
A: No. We reused the researchers' released detector and added dataset-preparation and evaluation scripts.

Q: Did you generate the mimicry attacks?
A: No. We used the released evasion graphs. We have not independently confirmed one-to-one matching between every attack and evasion file.

Q: Why five attack and evasion graphs?
A: To keep a reproducible midterm experiment manageable while troubleshooting and comparing training configurations. 
The released dataset contains 100 attack and 100 evasion graph files; we evaluated a subset.

Q: Why does batch71 have only a train folder?
A: We changed the benign training set to 71 graphs while reusing the same five attack and five evasion files from batch5, holding those test inputs fixed.

Q: What are model artifacts?
A: Saved intermediate outputs such as frequency data, thresholds, or cached computations used in subsequent evaluation. 
Our timed runs may reuse them.

Q: Why is evasion: 0 a successful attack outcome?
A: It is the count of evasion graphs detected. Zero detected out of five means all five escaped detection.

Q: Does more benign training always improve attack detection?
A: No. It changes the learned normal-pattern representation and potentially the scores and thresholds. 
We observed different results but did not isolate the cause.

Q: Why did training fpr: 0 differ from the held-out false-positive result?
A: The original output concerns training data, while our extra script evaluates previously unseen benign graphs. 
Generalization to unseen data is a different test, and our protocol may also differ from the paper's.

Q: How long will the commands take?
A: We measured about 26.84 seconds for the main Pagoda rerun and 30.60 seconds for the held-out evaluator on our laptop. Live execution times may vary.
